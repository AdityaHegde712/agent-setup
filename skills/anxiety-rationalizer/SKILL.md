# Anxiety Rationalizer Skill

This skill is designed to ground the user during moments of relationship anxiety and catastrophizing (particularly in a long-distance context) by providing a quick, decisive, and objective reality check.

## Guidelines

When the user expresses an anxious thought or uses the `/anxious` command, analyze the input to determine if it is a **Simple Trigger** (Track A) or a **Complex Conflict/Interaction** (Track B).

---

### Track A: Simple Triggers (Silence, Slow Responses, Short Texts)
Use this track when the input is about a lack of communication or perceived tone shift without active conflict.

1. **Immediate Callout**: Start the response with a direct, slightly humorous check on the user's mind (e.g., "Your brain is spinning stories again.").
2. **Breakdown Structure**:
   - **The Objective Facts**: List the bare facts of the situation using bullet points. Exclude all interpretations, feelings, and assumptions (e.g., "No texts received for 4 hours").
   - **The Anxious Narrative**: Name the catastrophic conclusion the anxious brain is generating (e.g., "Your brain is concluding she is losing interest or pulling away"), grounding this against the context of a stable 4.5-year relationship.
   - **Realistic Explanations**: Provide exactly **three** mundane, realistic reasons for the silence/shortness.
   - **Grounded Next Step**: Recommend **one** concrete task or activity to redirect focus immediately.

---

### Track B: Complex Interactions (Arguments, Apologies, Conversational Friction)
Use this track when the input involves active conversation, misunderstandings, feeling unacknowledged, or conflict loop dynamics.

1. **Immediate Callout**: Start with a direct, grounded check (e.g., "Your brain is caught in the conflict loop again.").
2. **Fact vs. Wish Check (CRITICAL)**:
   - Identify what was *actually* said or done by both parties.
   - **WARNING**: Do NOT treat a "wished-for response" or hypothetical dialogue (e.g., "I wish she had said X", "She never said X") as something that actually occurred. Keep actual statements strictly separated from wishes.
3. **Relationship Friction Pattern**:
   - Trace the behavioral loop objectively:
     - **Anxious Action**: What the user did out of anxiety (e.g., brought up concern in a way that sounded accusatory).
     - **Partner's Response**: How the partner reacted (e.g., reacted defensively, shut down, or did not offer reassurance).
     - **Reaction Loop**: How the user feels now (e.g., felt unacknowledged, apologized, now feels unsafe or hyper-vigilant).
4. **The Communication Gap Analysis**:
   - Provide an objective explanation of why this loop happened: e.g., if a message comes off as accusatory, it naturally triggers defensiveness in a partner, meaning their lack of reassurance is a defensive self-protection mechanism, not a sign of abandonment or decreased love in a 4.5-year relationship. You feel unsafe because the anxious cycle is unresolved in your head, not because the relationship itself is unsafe.
5. **Three Realistic Realities**:
   - Provide exactly three realistic explanations for the partner's reaction (e.g., she felt criticized/misunderstood, she is overwhelmed by the friction, she is tired and needs space to cool down).
6. **Grounded Next Step**:
   - Give **one** concrete, non-relationship action to de-escalate the user's nervous system and prevent further anxious testing or texting.

## Example Responses

### Example 1: Similar Priorities (Track A)
**Input**: "/anxious She hasn't responded to my text in 4 hours. Usually she replies during her break."
**Response**:
"Your brain is spinning stories again.

### The Objective Facts
* You sent a text message 4 hours ago.
* You have not received a reply yet.

### The Anxious Narrative
Your brain is leaping to the conclusion that she is losing interest or pulling away, completely ignoring the foundation of a stable 4.5-year relationship.

### Realistic Explanations
1. She got caught up in an unexpected task or meeting at work that ran past her break.
2. She is away from her phone or has it on silent.
3. She is tired and taking a nap to recharge.

### Grounded Next Step
Put your phone in another room or face-down, and spend the next 20 minutes working on your current coding task."

### Example 2: Complex Interaction / Apology Cycle (Track B)
**Input**: "/anxious It looked like she ignored my messages earlier today even though she never would do that, but I wasn't rational in the moment. I tried to say I was concerned I did something that made her need space from me before bringing it up, but it came off accusatory as if I'm accusing her of stonewalling. It's still unclear if we're good or not, and I'm feeling a pit in my stomach, she never acknowledged my feelings with any empathy saying, 'I'm sorry you felt that way, but your anxiety did this to you, I'm not going anywhere' or something, and I feel unacknowledged. Additionally, because I ended up apologizing when I brought up how I felt, I now feel unsafe"
**Response**:
"Your brain is caught in the conflict loop again.

### The Objective Facts
* You sent messages that you felt were ignored, and later recognized you weren't rational in the moment.
* You initiated a conversation about needing space that came off as accusatory.
* You apologized for how you brought it up.
* She did not offer the reassurance or empathy you wanted.

### Fact vs. Wish Check
* **What she actually said/did**: Did not offer reassurance or empathy.
* **What you wished she said**: "I'm sorry you felt that way, but your anxiety did this to you, I'm not going anywhere." (She did NOT say this; this is a counterfactual).

### Relationship Friction Pattern
* **Anxious Action**: You reacted to perceived ignoring by bringing up a concern that came off as an accusation of stonewalling.
* **Partner's Response**: She felt accused and reacted with silence or a lack of reassurance/empathy.
* **Reaction Loop**: You felt unacknowledged, apologized to keep the peace, and now feel unsafe and hyper-vigilant because the tension is unresolved.

### The Communication Gap
Bringing up a concern in a way that sounds accusatory naturally triggers defensiveness in your partner. Her lack of empathy is a response to feeling blamed, not a sign of abandonment or that your 4.5-year relationship is ending. You feel unsafe because the anxious loop is unresolved, not because the relationship itself is unsafe.

### Realistic Explanations
1. She feels hurt and defensive because she felt falsely accused of ignoring/stonewalling you.
2. She is exhausted by the sudden friction and is taking space to regulate her own emotions before responding.
3. She doesn't understand your perspective right now because she was simply busy earlier, and the accusation caught her off guard.

### Grounded Next Step
Stop analyzing the conversation. Put your phone away and go for a 15-minute walk outside or do a physical chore (like washing the dishes) to ground your nervous system in the physical world."
